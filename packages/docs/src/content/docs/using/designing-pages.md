Every page has a **Design**: how it looks, laid out visually in the page builder. You see the
page as visitors will, with your website's colours, fonts, navigation and footer, and change
it by clicking on it. New to the builder? Start with
[Getting around the builder](/using/getting-around-the-builder/).

## A new page

1. Go to **Content**, then **Pages**, and press **New page**.
2. Give the page a title.
3. Press **Return**, or **Start designing**. Lintel saves the page as a draft and opens it in
   the page builder.
4. The empty page says **Start building by adding a section, or design your own with
   elements**. **Add a section** opens **Insert** on **Sections**, and **Add elements** on
   **Elements**, with the search ready for you to type. **Start from a template**, under
   them, fills the page with a whole page from a template. Everything can be changed
   afterwards.

## A page you already have

The page's editor starts with its **Design** card: a small, live picture of the page and how
many sections it has. Press **Edit design** to open it in the page builder.

A page that hasn't been designed yet says so, with **Start from a template or section**.

The page's other details (its summary for search results, its address, where it sits among
your pages) are below the card, and in the panel on the right.

## In the page builder

- **Layers**, on the left, lists everything on the page. Pick something there or click it
  on the page to select it.
- **Insert**, beside Layers, is the library of things to add. See below.
- **Style**, **Content** and **Animate**, on the right, change the selected element: how it
  looks, what it says and how it moves.
- The page saves itself as you work. **Publish** puts it on your website.

### Insert

**Insert** has a search at the top and two kinds of things to add:

- **Sections**: ready-made parts of a page, such as a hero, three features, prices or
  opening times, in groups by what they're for, each with a small sketch. This is where
  Insert starts.
- **Elements**: single pieces, such as a heading, a paragraph, a picture or a button, as
  small tiles grouped by kind.

Groups open and close with their headings and say how many things they hold. Section groups
all start closed; among elements only the first is open. Lintel remembers which you leave
open. The search looks through sections and elements at once:
type a word and press the down arrow to move to the results. **Recently used** keeps what you
added last within easy reach.

Rest the pointer on anything, or move to it with the keyboard, to see what it's for. The ⓘ
button opens its details, with an **Insert** button. Press a section or element to add it
after whatever is selected, or drag it into **Layers** to put it exactly where you want.

### Style

**Style** is in groups: **Layout**, **Where it sits**, **Spacing**, **Size**, **Text**,
**Background**, **Corners and borders**, **Move and turn**, **Shape and mask**, **Position**,
**Effects** and **Blend and filters** (some only in **Advanced**; see
[Arranging things](/using/arranging-things/) and [Advanced styling](/using/advanced-styling/)).
Each group opens and closes with its heading. A closed group says what's set inside it, such
as "Side by side · gap M", so you can see the whole element at a glance.

In **Simple**, the groups people use most start open and Lintel remembers how you leave
them. In **Advanced**, every group starts closed except the one you used last.

Above the groups, **Which look you're changing** chooses whether you're styling the element
as it normally looks, or while it's hovered, pressed or focused. The site's own colours,
sizes, fonts and shared styles are under **Site styles**. Both are described in
[Site styles](/using/site-styles/).

**Content** works the same way for elements with a lot to fill in, such as a hero block:
**Words**, **Link**, **Picture** and **Options** each have their own group.

## Simple sections

Sites set up with an earlier version of Lintel may have **Page sections**: a form where a page
is built from blocks (a hero, text, a picture, a call to action, questions), one under
another. They keep working, and the website still shows them on pages without a design.

In a page's editor they're folded away under **Simple sections (no design)**, and only while
the page has no design. Once you design a page, the design is what the website shows.

Other content types can still have blocks fields, added under **Content types**.
